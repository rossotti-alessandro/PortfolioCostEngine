What we learned

Reading the numbers is solved. Pairing each number with its label is the hard part. The text layer and RapidOCR read {{value_recall:hybrid_route}} of the fee figures on the {{pages:hybrid_route}} shared pages. But no engine puts more than {{pair_recall:hybrid_vlm}} of them on the same row as their label. Most misses are figures whose label sits in a column or row heading, such as "Service costs → One-off → 0.00%". A bigger OCR model does not fix that. Table structure and a classification step do.

No single model wins. The cheap path (text layer, or OCR on scans, plus a layout model for tables) reaches {{pair_recall:hybrid_route}} at {{sec_per_page:hybrid_route}} s per page. The best vision model, PaddleOCR-VL, reaches {{pair_recall:paddleocr_vl}} at {{sec_per_page:paddleocr_vl}} s per page on this CPU. Combining the two gives {{pair_recall:hybrid_vlm}}. Most of that gain comes from scanned pages.

Recommended pipeline

1
Text layer first (pdfplumber, already in FeeLens)

Born-digital pages have exact text and exact word boxes for free, in {{sec_per_page:pdfplumber}} s per page. Every figure keeps its page and box as evidence.

2
Layout model for tables (PP-DocLayoutV3, or Heron, which is already inside Docling)

Finds {{table_roi_recall:ppdoclayout3}} of fee tables in about {{sec_per_page:ppdoclayout3}} s per page. Words inside a table region are rebuilt into rows. DocLayout-YOLO is about as good but AGPL, so we do not use it.

3
OCR only where there is no text (RapidOCR; Tesseract stays as fallback)

RapidOCR read {{value_recall:rapidocr}} of the figures with line boxes in {{sec_per_page:rapidocr}} s per page. That beats Tesseract ({{pair_recall:tesseract}} on the same rows) and docTR, which is slower.

4
Vision model as a second reader on scans and fee tables (PaddleOCR-VL 1.6)

It runs on the page regions that matter, not on every page. On 4 CPU cores it takes about a minute per page, so it fits a background job. A GPU would make it near real time. granite-docling (258M) is the light alternative; it gives boxes but scores lower.

5
Consensus, then review

A figure that both readers agree on is accepted: {{c:accepted}} figures, of which {{c:accepted_pct}} are true fees. Everything else goes to the review queue ({{c:review}} figures). A fee is never invented, and a figure is never set to zero because it is missing.

6
Classification step (rules first, then a local language model or the analyst)

Deciding which numbers are fees, and which fee type each one is, is the weakest link. The shared extractor that understands tables finds {{extract2_recall:hybrid_route}} of fees, but only {{extract2_precision:hybrid_route}} of what it keeps is a fee. This is where the next effort should go: a classifier per document type that uses row and column headings, run locally.

Which layout elements to detect

Every hand-checked figure is tagged by where it sits: a table, a key-value list (label and value on one line, no grid), running text, or a footnote. The scores per element decide what the layout step has to find.

  • Tables: detect them, and rebuild their rows and column headings. This is where fees are lost. The best pipeline puts only {{table_pair_recall:hybrid_vlm}} of table figures on the row of their label. Layout models find the table itself almost every time ({{table_roi_recall:ppdoclayout3}} for PP-DocLayoutV3), so the gap is the structure inside it: merged headings, a label in a column heading, a value that applies to a band of rows.
  • Key-value lists and text: no special model needed. The text layer already pairs most of them with their label (above 90% for key-value lines). Running text needs sentence-level reading ("a minimum of EUR 10 per order"), which is a classification job, not a layout one.
  • Footnotes: detect and attach to their table. They hold minimums, caps and exceptions. They are few but change the cost.
  • Page headers, page footers and logos only need to be recognised so that they are ignored.

Models added in this round

  • No new vision model beats PaddleOCR-VL. On the 8 hard pages, Infinity-Parser2, LightOnOCR-3, MinerU 2.5, MiniCPM-OCR and Xiaomi-OCR all put 79% of figures on their label's row, exactly as PaddleOCR-VL does, and they are 2 to 6 times slower on this CPU. They all read every value; the misses are labels that sit in a column or row heading, which no reader fixes. FireRed-OCR scores a little lower and is the slowest.
  • Layout and table structure: Egret and Heron-101 find tables as well as PP-DocLayoutV3. SLANet gives table structure in a few seconds per page. EasyOCR is slower and weaker than RapidOCR.
  • Not run: 22 models, each with its reason in the Models tab (licence, size, or remote code).

What the app does today

The FeeLens app itself is now one of the engines in the lab. On the two SYNTHETIC prospects it gets every pinned number right from the original PDFs. When the same documents are scanned, it loses a holdings page, so the totals are wrong, but its own checks raise the alarm. On the real public pages it reads only {{pair_recall:app_baseline}} of the fee figures, because its parsers were written for the synthetic layouts. That gap, not the OCR, is the first thing to close.

Scanned pages in the app were slow (minutes per page) because Tesseract's threads competed for the CPU. With one thread per call each page takes 4 to 9 seconds; that fix is its own story.

What we would not use

  • Asking a small general model for the fees directly (Qwen3-VL-2B): {{pair_recall:qwen3vl}} of fees paired with their label, {{sec_per_page:qwen3vl}} s per page, and loose boxes. Its {{extract_precision:qwen3vl}} precision is not enough for a figure we must source.
  • Fixed questions to a document QA model (LayoutLM): it found {{extract_recall:layoutlm_docqa}} of fees. Fee pages hold too many figures for one answer per question.
  • GOT-OCR 2.0 and Florence-2: they lose text on dense pages ({{value_recall:got_ocr}} and {{value_recall:florence2}} of figures read).
  • LightOnOCR-2 and GLM-OCR: they read text well ({{value_recall:lighton_ocr}}, {{value_recall:glm_ocr}}) but give no boxes, so a figure could not be shown on the page as evidence. They are also slower than PaddleOCR-VL here.

Limits

There are 54 pages with 813 hand-checked figures (63 pages with the simulated scans), and the vision models ran on {{pages:paddleocr_vl}} of those pages ({{gt_items:paddleocr_vl}} figures). That is enough to rank the engines, not to quote an exact accuracy. All engines ran on a CPU with no GPU, and the speeds are for that machine. The documents are public; no client document was used. The simulated scans are marked as simulated in the Documents view.
